History

Colonial Era

Spanish colonization, missionaries, shipbuilding, and pirate raids in Guimaras.

  1. Home
  2. Colonial Era

The colonial history of Guimaras began in 1521, when Spanish explorers passed near the island after the defeat of Ferdinand Magellan. It is believed that the island was named "Guimaraez" by a Portuguese sailor.

By around 1581, Spanish Governor Gonzalo Ronquillo de Peñalosa established a settlement in Guimaras to Christianize the natives. Small villages called pueblocitos were organized, such as Nayup under Saint Peter the Apostle and Igang under Saint Anne.

The spread of Christianity continued through the efforts of different religious groups. The Augustinians first established visitas (chapelries) under Oton, Iloilo. Later, the Jesuits took over, followed by the Dominicans, who managed the island's spiritual administration. By 1755, Guimaras was organized into a regular parish.

During Spanish rule, the island was known for its rich natural resources such as wood, stone, wax, and honey. The people were skilled in shipbuilding, while agriculture and fishing became the main sources of livelihood, producing rice, corn, tobacco, coffee, and cacao.

Despite these developments, Guimaras suffered frequent pirate raids, which slowed population growth. Over time, as the population increased, the island was granted municipal status, with the seat of government established in Tilad (now Buenavista).
